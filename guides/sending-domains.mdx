# Sending Domains

You can send from a Nitrosend address such as
`hello@your-brand.nitrosend.net`, or connect a domain you own. A Nitrosend address
needs no DNS setup and does not use your custom-domain allowance.

## Use your Nitrosend address

Choose an address during setup and activate it. If you complete your Brand Kit
first, Nitrosend can suggest a name from your company name. Activating an address
does not send any email. An existing custom sender stays selected until you
choose to replace it.

Under **Settings → Domains**, the **Nitrosend address** section appears below your
custom domains. Select **Activate sender** if needed, or **Use this sender** to
make it your default.

The shared `nitrosend.net` sender is paced across the service at 20 recipients
per second, with a 5-per-second share for each account across its brands.
Small bursts are allowed. Individual flow and transactional messages receive
priority headroom ahead of campaigns. Queued mail continues automatically;
these rates are separate from your plan's rolling 24-hour allowance.

For sustained campaign volume, connect your own sending subdomain. It does
not use the shared-domain rate pool; your plan allowance and other provider
and safety limits still apply. Upgrading alone does not remove shared-domain pacing.

## Change your Nitrosend address

In **Settings → Domains**, select **Change address name** under your Nitrosend
address. You can also make this change from **Inbox → Manage inboxes**.
For example, `hello@old-name.nitrosend.net` can become
`hello@new-name.nitrosend.net`.

The same inbox keeps its messages and attachments. Previous addresses keep
receiving mail, and new replies use the new address. Past emails stay unchanged.
Old names remain reserved to your brand, so another customer cannot claim them.
System names and names that impersonate other businesses are unavailable.

Address changes share your existing address allowance. You can claim at most two
new names per brand per hour, including the first address. A campaign, flow or
reply still using the address must finish or be updated before you change it.
The new sender goes through the usual sending checks.

For API clients, send `subdomain` and the current `brand_subdomain.fqdn` as
`expected_fqdn` to `POST /v1/my/brands/{sid}/rename_hosted_sender`. An exact retry
returns the same address. The response is the updated brand. The agent tool uses
`nitro_manage_domains` with `operation: "rename_brand_subdomain"`, the same two
parameters, and `confirm: true`.

## Add a sending subdomain

1. Go to **Settings → Domains** ([app.nitrosend.com/my/settings/domains](https://app.nitrosend.com/my/settings/domains)).
2. Under **Add a sending domain**, enter a subdomain you own — for example
   `send.yourdomain.com`.
3. Nitrosend generates the DNS records for that subdomain and shows them on screen.
4. Add those records at your DNS provider (see below). Nitrosend verifies them
   automatically — once they resolve, the domain flips to **Verified** and becomes
   available as a send-from address.

## Use a subdomain, not your primary domain

<Warning>
  Always send from a subdomain (`send.yourdomain.com`), never your bare apex /
  primary domain (`yourdomain.com`).
</Warning>

A subdomain **isolates** your marketing and product sending — its DNS records and
its sending reputation — from the domain that runs your everyday inbox (for
example Google Workspace or Gmail on `yourdomain.com`).

That matters for two reasons:

- **It keeps your corporate mail untouched.** Your primary domain already has mail
  authentication set up for your team's inboxes. Sending bulk email from it can
  interfere with that setup — a subdomain leaves it alone.
- **It protects your reputation.** Sending volume builds a reputation with inbox
  providers. Keeping it on a dedicated subdomain means a bad send never drags down
  the domain your team emails from.

This is the setup inbox providers like Gmail expect from senders.

## Add the DNS records

The **Domains** screen lists the exact records for your subdomain: typically a
verification record, **SPF**, **DKIM**, and a return-path / tracking record. Add
each one at your DNS provider (Cloudflare, GoDaddy, Namecheap, Route 53, and so on).

Enter each record's **Host** in your provider's Host or Name field, with the type
and value as shown. Most providers add your domain to that field themselves, so
`send` becomes `send.yourdomain.com`, and `@` means the domain itself. Some
providers, such as Cloudflare, accept the full name too.

<Info>
  DNS changes can take anywhere from a few minutes to a couple of days to
  propagate. Nitrosend re-checks automatically — you don't need to trigger
  verification manually.
</Info>

## Share a subdomain with another brand or client

The account owner or an administrator can share an exact subdomain of a verified
domain. For example, the owner of `alvin.finance` can give one brand
`envoy.alvin.finance` and another `travel.alvin.finance`.

1. Open **Settings → Domains** in the brand that owns the verified domain.
2. Select **Share a subdomain**, enter a name such as `envoy`, and choose the
   destination brand or eligible managed client.
3. Select **Share subdomain**, then **Open domain setup** to finish the usual DNS
   setup in that brand. Only the new subdomain's records need to change.
4. Open **Inbox → Manage inboxes**. If receiving is not configured yet, choose
   **New inbox → Set up another domain**, select the verified shared subdomain,
   and finish receiving setup. Add its MX record only on that subdomain.
5. Add further named inboxes on the same subdomain from **New inbox**.

The new brand has its own sending identity and inboxes. Sharing does not switch
an existing sender, expose another brand's messages, or reuse its sending history.
The sending domain (`send.envoy.alvin.finance`) and visible From domain
(`envoy.alvin.finance`) use one custom-domain slot in the destination account.
Inboxes use that account's existing inbox allowance and paid capacity.

Managed clients still use the existing ownership claim and client-paid
subscription. Sharing a domain does not bypass those requirements.

To withdraw permission, open the same section and choose **Stop sharing**.
Subsequent sends, including queued mail, and receiving stop for that subdomain.
Existing messages stay in their original brand; other subdomains keep working.
**Share again** restores permission after the owner confirms the same destination.
If a managed relationship ends, a new relationship needs fresh sharing permission.

For API clients, the owner brand uses:

- `GET /v1/my/domains/{domain_id}/subdomains` to list shared children (25 per page).
- `POST /v1/my/domains/{domain_id}/subdomains` with
  `{ "subdomain": { "subdomain": "envoy", "brand_sid": "destination-brand-sid" } }`.
- `DELETE /v1/my/domains/{domain_id}/subdomains/{id}` to stop sharing.

The response identifies the child sending domain. Use the destination brand's
credentials for its normal domain and inbox setup. Brand-pinned keys do not
receive a directory of other brands. An exact creation retry reuses the same
claim. Agents use the same sharing actions, with confirmation before changes.

## Route different mail through different subdomains

You can verify more than one subdomain and push different email through each by
changing the send-from address:

- `hello@news.yourdomain.com` — marketing campaigns
- `hello@txn.yourdomain.com` — transactional / product email

Splitting sending this way isolates reputation between mail types, so a large
promotional send can't drag down your receipts and password resets. This is mostly
useful for **large-volume senders** — most brands are perfectly fine with a single
subdomain.

## Set a reply-to address

Your send-from address doesn't have to be where replies land. Set a **Reply-To**
under **Brand → Sender** ([app.nitrosend.com/my/brand/sender](https://app.nitrosend.com/my/brand/sender)) and replies go to whatever inbox you like — independent of
the subdomain you send from.

For example, send from `news@send.yourdomain.com` but have replies arrive at
`hello@yourdomain.com`. The Reply-To can be any mailbox you actually monitor, on
any domain.

## How sending capacity grows

Sending allowance depends on your plan and sender standing: Probation, Standard
or Trusted. Standing ranks attributable delivery history, positive current list
verification, domain strength and account signals together. Strong evidence can
earn a higher standing on day one; payment alone cannot. Trusted receives the
full plan allowance immediately, without a second volume ramp. See
[Pricing](/pricing) for the plan-and-standing allowances.

Each effective sending identity still owns its own delivery and reputation
history. History is not copied between domains, and changing sender context
does not copy its first-send content clearance. Probation continues to require
review of each changed content identity; higher standing does not bypass sender
authentication, suppression or safety checks.

The account's fixed plan/standing allowance, provider limits, and delivery
pacing are separate inputs to one admission decision. The API, MCP status, and
review responses report the binding result; clients should not calculate a
private limit from plan names.
